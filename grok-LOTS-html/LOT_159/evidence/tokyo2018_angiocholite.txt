Charcot's triad has high specificity but low sensitivity. Recent multicenter series found the triad in 26.4 percent and 21.2 percent of patients. Older series reported sensitivity on the order of 50 to 70 percent.

TG18 adopts the TG13 diagnostic criteria. A diagnosis requires three pathologies: systemic inflammation (must be present), cholestasis, and bile duct lesions on imaging.
Systemic inflammation: fever (body temperature above 38 degrees Celsius) or an inflammatory response (white cell count under 4 or over 10 in the table unit, C-reactive protein at least 1 mg/dL).
Cholestasis: jaundice with total bilirubin at least 2 mg/dL, or liver tests above 1.5 times the standard (ALP, GGT, AST, ALT).
Imaging shows biliary dilatation, a stone, a stricture or a stent.

Grade I, mild: does not meet grade II or grade III. Antibiotics are usually enough. Drainage if no response.
Grade II, moderate: early biliary drainage. At least two of: white cells at least 12,000 or under 4,000 per the stated unit, temperature at least 39 degrees Celsius, age at least 75 years, total bilirubin at least 5 mg/dL, albumin below 0.73 times the lower limit of normal.
Grade III, severe: any one organ dysfunction. Cardiovascular: dopamine at least 5 micrograms per kilogram per minute or noradrenaline. Neurological: disturbed consciousness. Respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 ratio under 300. Renal: oliguria or serum creatinine above 2.0 mg/dL. Hepatic: PT-INR above 1.5. Coagulation: the extracted platelet threshold is printed as an ambiguous « 10 4 per microlitre » and is not rewritten as a cleaned number. Urgent biliary drainage and organ support.

The diagnostic rate of TG13 criteria applied retrospectively was 90.0 percent (5,454 of 6,063) versus 79.4 percent for TG07.
