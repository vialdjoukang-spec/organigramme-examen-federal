Diagnosis of alcohol-related liver disease is usually suspected upon documentation of regular alcohol consumption of >20 g/d in females and >30 g/d in males together with clinical and/or biological abnormalities suggestive of liver injury.

AST levels are rarely above 300 U/L, while serum ALT levels are commonly lower. The AST/ALT ratio, typically greater than 1, is neither specific nor sensitive, particularly in the cirrhotic stage. The laboratory profile of alcoholic hepatitis reveals neutrophilia, hyperbilirubinemia (>50 µmol/L), serum levels of AST greater than twice the upper limit of normal, AST greater than 50 IU/L although rarely above 300 IU/L, with an AST/ALT ratio typically greater than 1.5-2.0.

The modified Maddrey discriminant function, modified in 1989, uses a cut-off value of 32 to identify severe alcoholic hepatitis. Patients with mDF <32 had a less than 10 percent risk of one-month mortality. Table 6 shows that the Maddrey score uses bilirubin and prothrombin time or INR. The Glasgow alcoholic hepatitis score was derived from age, serum bilirubin, blood urea, prothrombin time and white blood cell count. The GAHS ranges from 5 to 12. Patients with an mDF ≥32 and a GAHS ≥9 have a poor prognosis. The MELD score above 20 was associated with a high risk of 90-day mortality in retrospective studies of alcoholic hepatitis.

The Lille score ranges from 0 to 1. A score ≥0.45 indicates non-response to corticosteroids. Three patterns: complete responders (Lille ≤0.16), partial responders (0.16-0.56) and null responders (Lille ≥0.56). Corticosteroids should be discontinued in null responders.

In the absence of active infection, corticosteroids (prednisolone 40 mg/day or methylprednisolone 32 mg/day) should be considered in severe alcoholic hepatitis. N-acetylcysteine for five days, intravenously, may be combined with corticosteroids. Abstinence is the cornerstone. Energy intake 35-40 kcal/kg and protein 1.2-1.5 g/kg are the ESPEN targets cited by the guideline. STOPAH reported a borderline reduction in 28-day mortality with prednisolone 40 mg/day and no benefit after one month.
