EASL 2017 Clinical Practice Guidelines on the management of hepatitis B virus infection. Journal of Hepatology 2017, volume 67, pages 370-398. Publié en ligne le 18 avril 2017. DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2017.03.021. Extraits lus le 31 octobre 2026.

Chronic HBV infection is classified into five phases: (I) HBeAg-positive chronic infection, (II) HBeAg-positive chronic hepatitis, (III) HBeAg-negative chronic infection, (IV) HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis, (V) HBsAg-negative phase.

Phase 1, formerly called immune tolerant: serum HBeAg, very high HBV DNA, ALT persistently normal (traditional ULN approximately 40 IU/L), minimal or no necroinflammation. Highly contagious. More prolonged after perinatal infection.
Phase 2: HBeAg, high HBV DNA, elevated ALT, moderate or severe necroinflammation, accelerated fibrosis.
Phase 3, formerly inactive carrier: anti-HBe, undetectable or low HBV DNA (under 2,000 IU/ml), normal ALT. Some have DNA above 2,000 IU/ml usually under 20,000 IU/ml with persistently normal ALT. Spontaneous HBsAg loss 1 to 3 percent per year, often with HBsAg under 1,000 IU/ml.
Phase 4: no HBeAg, usually anti-HBe, persistent or fluctuating moderate to high HBV DNA, elevated ALT.
Phase 5, occult infection: HBsAg negative, anti-HBc positive, with or without anti-HBs, ALT usually normal, serum HBV DNA usually undetectable, cccDNA often still in the liver.

Treat when HBV DNA is above 2,000 IU/ml and ALT is above the ULN and/or histology shows at least moderate necroinflammation or fibrosis. Treat any detectable HBV DNA in compensated or decompensated cirrhosis, regardless of ALT. Treat when HBV DNA is above 20,000 IU/ml and ALT is above twice the ULN, without requiring a biopsy. HBeAg-positive infection with persistently normal ALT may be treated after 30 years. Family history of HCC or cirrhosis, and extrahepatic manifestations, are additional reasons to treat.

Preferred long-term treatment: entecavir, tenofovir disoproxil or tenofovir alafenamide. The main endpoint is sustained suppression of HBV DNA. HBsAg loss is optimal.
