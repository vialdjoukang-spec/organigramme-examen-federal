Figge J, Jabor A, Kazda A, Fencl V. Anion gap and hypoalbuminemia. Critical Care Medicine. 1998;26(11):1807-1810. PMID 9824071. Résumé lu le 31 octobre 2026.

Each g/L decrease in serum albumin caused the observed anion gap to underestimate the total concentration of gap anions by 0.25 mEq/L.

The observed anion gap can be adjusted for the effect of abnormal serum albumin concentrations as follows: adjusted anion gap = observed anion gap + 0.25 x ([normal albumin] - [observed albumin]), where albumin concentrations are in g/L; if given in g/dL, the factor is 2.5.

Marked hypoalbuminemia was common: 49% of the critically ill patients had serum albumin concentration of <20 g/L.
