Jonklaas J, Bianco AC, Bauer AJ et al. Guidelines for the Treatment of Hypothyroidism. Prepared by the American Thyroid Association Task Force on Thyroid Hormone Replacement. Thyroid. 2014. DOI 10.1089/thy.2014.0028. Lu le 31 octobre 2026.

Coma myxœdémateux. Initial thyroid hormone replacement should be levothyroxine given intravenously. A loading dose of 200 to 400 micrograms of levothyroxine may be given, with lower doses for smaller or older patients and those with a history of coronary disease or arrhythmia. A daily replacement dose of 1.6 microgram per kilogram, reduced to 75 percent as long as it is given intravenously, can be given thereafter. Empiric glucocorticoid coverage should be employed as part of the initial therapy, with intravenous glucocorticoid administration at doses appropriate for the stressed state, preceding levothyroxine. Supportive measures treat hypothermia, hypoventilation, volume depletion, hypoglycemia and hyponatremia. Therapy should not be delayed while waiting for blood test results. The degree of TSH elevation may not indicate the severity. Liothyronine, if used, loading 5 to 20 micrograms then 2.5 to 10 micrograms every 8 hours, lower doses if the patient is small, old, or has coronary disease. High serum triiodothyronine during treatment has been associated with mortality.

Syndrome de basse T3, ou nonthyroidal illness syndrome. Circulating T3 decreases, followed by declines in serum TSH and or T4, then free T4 in some sicker patients. The guideline recommends against levothyroxine therapy for hospitalized patients with this syndrome, and against liothyronine therapy for the same patients. A rise in TSH during worsening critical illness in a patient with known hypothyroidism suggests thyroid hormone deficiency, which is a different situation.

Hypothyroïdie déjà substituée et maladie grave. Patients with established hypothyroidism are maintained on thyroid hormone when they become critically ill.
