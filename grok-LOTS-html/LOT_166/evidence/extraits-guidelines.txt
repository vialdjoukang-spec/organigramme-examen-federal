Pleural fluid is an exudate if one or more of the following criteria are met:
Pleural fluid protein divided by serum protein is >0.5
Pleural fluid lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) divided by serum LDH is >0.6
Pleural fluid LDH >2/3 the upper limits of laboratory normal value for serum LDH
If pleural fluid pH is ≤7.2, this implies a high risk of CPPE or pleural infection and an intercostal drain should be inserted if the volume of accessible pleural fluid on ultrasound makes it safe to do so.
If pleural fluid pH is >7.2 and <7.4, pleural fluid LDH should be measured and if >900 IU/L intercostal drainage should be considered.
If pleural fluid pH is ≥7.4, this implies a low risk and there is no indication for immediate drainage.
an initial pleural fluid glucose <3.3 mmol/L may be used as an indicator of high probability of CPPE.
The presence of macroscopically purulent pleural fluid is termed empyema.
Image-guided thoracentesis should always be used to reduce the risk of complications.
25–50 mL of pleural fluid should be submitted for cytological analysis in patients with suspected malignant pleural effusion.
Initial drainage of pleural infection should be undertaken using a small bore chest tube (14F or smaller).
