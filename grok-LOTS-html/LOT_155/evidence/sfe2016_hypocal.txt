Severe hypocalcaemia: serum calcium <1.9 mmol/l and/or symptomatic at any level below the reference range.

This is a medical emergency.

Symptoms typically develop when adjusted serum calcium falls below about 1.9 mmol/l, but this threshold varies and depends on the rate of fall.

Initial treatment read: 10 to 20 ml of 10% calcium gluconate in 50 to 100 ml of 5% dextrose, intravenous, over 10 minutes, with ECG monitoring. This can be repeated until the patient is asymptomatic.

Then an infusion: 100 ml of 10% calcium gluconate diluted in 1 litre of saline or 5% dextrose, infused at 50 to 100 ml per hour. The lesson does not rewrite this rate as ml/h in the fiche, but the volume and the duration are those of the guidance.

Calcium chloride is an alternative and is more irritant to veins. The guidance says it should only be given via a central line.

Titrate to normocalcaemia and continue until treatment of the underlying cause has taken effect.

In post-operative hypocalcaemia and hypoparathyroidism, the guidance starts alfacalcidol or calcitriol. The microgram dose of that sentence is not copied, because it was not read in a Swiss professional information for this lesson.

The signs of Chvostek and Trousseau were not in the extract read. They are not stated in the lesson.
