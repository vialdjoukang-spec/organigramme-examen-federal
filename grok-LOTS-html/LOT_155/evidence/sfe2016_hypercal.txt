Emergency management of acute hypercalcaemia in adult patients.
Endocrine Connections. 2016. DOI 10.1530/EC-16-0055.
Passage lu le 31 octobre 2026.

Serum calcium is bound to albumin, and measurements should be adjusted for serum albumin.

Below 3.0 mmol/l, hypercalcaemia is often asymptomatic and does not usually require urgent correction.

Between 3.0 and 3.5 mmol/l, it may be well tolerated if it has risen slowly, but it may be symptomatic and prompt treatment is usually indicated.

Above 3.5 mmol/l, urgent correction is required due to the risk of dysrhythmia and coma.

Clinical features read: polyuria and thirst, anorexia, nausea and constipation, mood disturbance, cognitive dysfunction, confusion and coma, renal impairment.

Also read: shortened QT interval and dysrhythmias, nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, pancreatitis, peptic ulceration, hypertension, cardiomyopathy, muscle weakness, band keratopathy.

Ninety percent of hypercalcaemia is due to primary hyperparathyroidism or malignancy.

Less common causes read: thiazide diuretics, familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia, non-malignant granulomatous disease, thyrotoxicosis, tertiary hyperparathyroidism, hypervitaminosis D, rhabdomyolysis, lithium, immobilisation, adrenal insufficiency, milk-alkali syndrome, hypervitaminosis A, theophylline toxicity, phaeochromocytoma.

Examination: cognitive impairment, fluid balance, neck, respiratory system, abdomen, breasts, lymph nodes.

ECG: shortened QT interval or other conduction abnormalities.

Bloods: calcium adjusted for albumin, phosphate, parathyroid hormone, urea and electrolytes.

High calcium and high parathyroid hormone point to primary or tertiary hyperparathyroidism. Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia may be misdiagnosed as primary hyperparathyroidism.

High calcium and low parathyroid hormone point to malignancy or other less common causes.

Rehydration read: intravenous 0.9% saline, 4 to 6 litres in 24 hours. Monitor fluid overload if renal impairment or in the elderly.

Loop diuretics are rarely used and only if fluid overload develops. They are not effective for reducing serum calcium.

Intravenous bisphosphonate is the next step in the guidance. The lesson copies the milligram dose only from the Swiss professional information of the named product.

Glucocorticoids inhibit calcitriol production in lymphoma, other granulomatous diseases or vitamin D poisoning. The milligram figure written in this guidance is not copied.

Calcimimetics, denosumab and calcitonin are second line, under specialist supervision.

Parathyroidectomy can be considered in acute primary hyperparathyroidism if hypercalcaemia is severe and other measures fail.
