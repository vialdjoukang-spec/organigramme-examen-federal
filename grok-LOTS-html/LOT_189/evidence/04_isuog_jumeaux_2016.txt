The donor twin has a DVP of < 2 cm (oligohydramnios) and the recipient twin has a DVP > 8 cm (polyhydramnios). In Europe, the diagnosis of polyhydramnios is made when DVP ≥ 8 cm at ≤ 20 weeks and ≥ 10 cm after 20 weeks' gestation. TTTS affects 10-15% of monochorionic twin pregnancies. If untreated, it leads to fetal demise in up to 90% of cases, with morbidity rates in survivors of over 50%. Laser treatment results in 60-70% double survival and 80-90% survival of at least one twin.

Quintero staging remains the classification system of choice. Stage I: polyhydramnios-oligohydramnios sequence, donor bladder visible. Stage II: bladder in donor twin not visible. Stage III: absent or reversed umbilical artery diastolic flow, reversed ductus venosus a-wave, or pulsatile umbilical venous flow. Stage IV: hydrops in one or both twins. Stage V: death of one or both twins. Laser ablation is the treatment of choice for stages II and above. Conservative management or laser can be considered for stage I. When laser is not available, serial amnioreduction is an acceptable alternative after 26 weeks. Screening for TTTS should start at 16 weeks and be repeated every 2 weeks. In uncomplicated monochorionic twins, scans every 2 weeks from 16 weeks. In uncomplicated dichorionic twins, every 4 weeks after the anomaly scan around 20 weeks.

TAPS antenatal stage 1: donor MCA-PSV > 1.5 MoM and recipient MCA-PSV < 1.0 MoM, without other signs of fetal compromise. Stage 2: donor > 1.7 MoM and recipient < 0.8 MoM. Stage 3: stage 1 or 2 plus cardiac compromise in the donor. Stage 4: hydrops of the donor. Stage 5: death of one or both fetuses, preceded by TAPS. Postnatal hemoglobin difference > 8.0 g/dL defines stage 1, > 11.0 stage 2, > 14.0 stage 3, > 17.0 stage 4, > 20.0 stage 5. Spontaneous TAPS incidence is up to 5% of monochorionic diamniotic twins, and up to 13% after laser for TTTS. The prenatal diagnosis uses discordant MCA-PSV, including MCA-PSV > 1.5 multiples of the median in the donor, suggesting fetal anemia.

After laser, recurrence of TTTS is up to 14%. Cerebral abnormalities were reported in 5% after laser, 14% after serial amnioreduction and 21% after expectant management. Pregnancy loss after chorionic villus sampling in twins was 3.8%, and after amniocentesis 3.1%, in one meta-analysis cited by the guideline.
