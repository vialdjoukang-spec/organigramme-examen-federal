Muriithi AK, Nasr SH, Leung N. Utility of urine eosinophils in the diagnosis of acute interstitial nephritis. Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology 2013;8:1857-1862. doi 10.2215/CJN.01330213. Reçu le 3 février 2013, accepté le 19 juin 2013.
Texte lu le 31 octobre 2026. URL https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3817898/

Hansel stain. At a 1% cutoff, sensitivity 30.8% and specificity 68.2% for biopsy-proven acute interstitial nephritis versus all other diagnoses. Positive likelihood ratio 0.97. Negative likelihood ratio 1.01.
At a 5% cutoff, sensitivity 19.8% and specificity 91.2%.
91 patients had acute interstitial nephritis, 80% drug induced, among 566 patients with a urine eosinophil test and a native kidney biopsy within a week.
Urine eosinophils were found in a variety of kidney diseases besides acute interstitial nephritis. At the 1% cutoff the test does not shift pretest probability. Even at 5%, it performed poorly in distinguishing acute interstitial nephritis from acute tubular necrosis.
