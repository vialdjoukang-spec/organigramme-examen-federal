When granular casts are dense and brownish or burnt umber, they are called muddy brown casts. When hospitalized patients with acute kidney injury have large numbers of these casts, they are thought to be pathognomonic for severe acute tubular necrosis. Granular casts can also be seen with acute interstitial nephritis, thrombotic microangiopathy, and other kidney lesions.
Dysmorphic red blood cells making up more than 5% of total red blood cells support glomerular bleeding. One study demonstrated 12.4% acanthocytes in biopsy-proven glomerular disease.
Circular fat droplets containing cholesterol esters produce birefringent Maltese crosses under polarized light. Oval fat bodies are macrophages or renal tubular epithelial cells engorged with fat. They can be embedded in a cast, forming a fatty cast.
Waxy casts are described among acellular casts in chronic disease.
Nephritic sediment: large number of erythrocytes and red-cell casts.

Uric acid: amber rhomboids, barrels, rosettes, needles, six-sided plates; pH 5.4-5.8; strong polychromatic.
Cystine: colorless hexagonal plates with irregular sides; pH 5.5; weak.
Leucine: yellow-brown spheres with concentric striations; pH 5.5-6.5; Maltese cross.
2,8-dihydroxyadenine: reddish-brown round with central spicules; pH 5.5-7.0; Maltese cross.
Tyrosine: colorless to yellow thin needles in bundles or rosettes; pH 5.5-6.5; strong.
Cholesterol: thin plates with well-defined edges; pH 5.5; negative birefringence.
Ammonium biurate: yellow-brown spheres with spicules, thorn apples; pH 5.5-7.0; strong.
Calcium carbonate: dumbbells, thick rods, four-leaf clover; pH 7.0; strong.
Bilirubin crystals: yellow needle-like, attach to cell surfaces; pH 5.5; weak.
Sulfadiazine: amber shocks or sheaves of wheat, shells; pH 5.5; strong.
Acyclovir: thin needles; pH 5.5-7.0; strong.
Atazanavir: thin needles; pH 6.0-7.0; strong.
Methotrexate: yellow-brown; pH 5.4-6.0; strong.
Vitamin C as calcium oxalate monohydrate; pH 5.4-6.7; strong.
Triamterene: brown spheres, also green, orange or red; pH 5.5; Maltese cross.
Ciprofloxacin: colorless needles, stars, fans, sheaves; pH > 7.0; strong.
Amoxicillin: colorless thin needles, broom or brush-like; pH 5.5-6.5; strong.
Crystals may precipitate if the urine stands more than 2 hours, including uric acid in healthy people.
