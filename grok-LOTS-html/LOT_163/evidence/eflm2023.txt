Diagnostic limits such as 10 x 10^6 cells/L at urine concentrations comparable to morning specimens only: urine osmolality > 300 mOsm/kgH2O, or urine density by refractometry > 1.015. With a dilute urine, a false negative case is to be considered.

For mid-stream specimens, a cut-off from 10^2 CFU/mL to 10^5 CFU/mL has been noted, depending on symptoms (Stamm WE et al, N Engl J Med 1982;307:463).

Nitrite test has a high specificity, while the leukocyte esterase test improves the sensitivity. Meta-analysis of 72 studies (Devillé et al, BMC Urol 2004): combined sensitivity of either leukocyte esterase or nitrite was 80% against 10^5 CFU/mL, 67% against 10^4 CFU/mL, and 45% against 10^3 CFU/mL.

Analytical sensitivity of the test strip for blood is about 80% at 10 x 10^6 RBC/L against particle counting. Specificity is reduced because RBC lyse easily, and specimens may contain haemoglobin from in vivo haemolysis or myoglobin in rhabdomyolysis.

Casts are formed in the distal tubules and collecting ducts from Tamm-Horsfall glycoprotein (uromodulin).
Hyaline casts: concentrated morning urine, dehydration, or strenuous exercise, and also renal disease.
Granular casts: suggest the presence of a renal disease.
Waxy casts: usually large, clear-cut edges, refractile, homogeneous, resembling wax. Found in patients with chronic renal insufficiency or failure.
Fatty casts: contain lipid particles. Typical in patients with heavy proteinuria.
Erythrocyte casts: always indicating bleeding from the renal parenchyma.
Leukocyte casts: usually granulocytes; indicate acute pyelonephritis, interstitial nephritis, or proliferative glomerulonephritis.
Tubular epithelial cell casts: acute tubular necrosis, acute interstitial nephritis, acute cellular rejection, glomerular disorders.
Haemoglobin and myoglobin casts: brownish, granular surface, cannot be differentiated from each other by morphology.
Lipids: polarised light, seen as Maltese crosses. Oval fat bodies are lipid-laden tubular epithelial cells or macrophages.

Uric acid crystals: acidic urine only (pH < 5.8), amber, birefringent.
Calcium oxalate dihydrate: bipyramidal. Calcium oxalate monohydrate: ovoid, dumb-bell, brightly birefringent. May be confused with RBC.
Triple phosphate: transparent birefringent prisms, usually with a coffin lid appearance.
Cystine: thin hexagonal birefringent plates. Precipitation increased at low pH (< 6).
Drug crystals: sulphadiazine (sheaves of wheat), acyclovir (needles), indinavir, ciprofloxacin, amoxycillin.

Isomorphic RBC indicate bleeding from the urinary tract, whereas dysmorphic RBC suggest glomerular bleeding. Acanthocytes (G1 cells) are cited from Kohler 1991.

Basic versus advanced microscopy: basic level groups casts as hyaline versus non-hyaline. Advanced level separates erythrocyte casts, granulocyte casts, waxy casts, fatty casts, and rare crystals including drugs and cystine.
