Perazella MA, Coca SG, Kanbay M, Brewster UC, Parikh CR. Diagnostic value of urine microscopy for differential diagnosis of acute kidney injury in hospitalized patients. Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology 2008;3:1615-1619. doi 10.2215/CJN.02860608.
Texte lu le 31 octobre 2026. URL https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2572284/

Urinary microscopy in patients with acute tubular necrosis classically is described as containing renal tubular epithelial cells, renal tubular epithelial cell casts, granular casts, and muddy brown or mixed cellular casts, whereas sediment in patients with prerenal acute kidney injury usually demonstrates occasional hyaline or fine granular casts.
In patients with a high pretest probability of acute tubular necrosis, any granular casts or renal tubular epithelial cells (score of 2 or more in that paper) gave a positive predictive value of 100% and a negative predictive value of 44% for a final diagnosis of acute tubular necrosis.
In patients initially thought to have prerenal acute kidney injury, lack of granular casts or renal tubular epithelial cells had a sensitivity of 0.73 and a specificity of 0.75, and a negative predictive value of 91%.
The odds ratio for acute tubular necrosis was 9.7 (95% confidence interval 5.3 to 18.6) for score 1 versus 0, and 74.0 (16.6 to 329.1) for score of 2 or more versus 0.
