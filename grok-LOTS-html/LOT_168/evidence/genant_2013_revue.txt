Revue lue le 31.10.2026. Recognizing and reporting osteoporotic vertebral fractures. PMC3591834. Les grades sont ceux de la méthode de Genant, article princeps Genant et al., Journal of Bone and Mineral Research, 1993, volume 8, pages 1137 et suivantes.

Thoracic and lumbar vertebrae from T4 to L4 are graded on visual inspection and without direct vertebral measurement as normal (grade 0), mildly deformed (grade 1: reduction of 20-25% of height and 10-20% of projected vertebral area), moderately deformed (grade 2: reduction of 26-40% of height and 21-40% of projected vertebral area), and severely deformed (grade 3: reduction of more than 40% of height and projected vertebral area). A grade 0.5 designates borderline vertebrae. The type of deformity (wedge, biconcavity, or compression) is not linked to the grade. Vertebral deformities due to degenerative changes should be ruled out. Normal vertebrae in the middle thoracic spine, especially in women, and at the thoracolumbar junction, especially in men, are slightly more wedged. Intraobserver agreement 93-99% and interobserver agreement 90-99% are reported for the method in experienced use.
