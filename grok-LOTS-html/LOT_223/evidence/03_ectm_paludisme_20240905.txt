Swiss Expert Committee for Travel Medicine. Malaria prevention / prophylaxis.
Last updated: 05.09.2024. Texte lu le 1er novembre 2026.
URL : https://www.healthytravel.ch/get-file?attachment_id=2428&download_file=EKRM_FLYER_PROFs_EN_Malaria_2024.pdf

Malaria is a life-threatening infection, transmitted by mosquitoes that bite from dusk to dawn.
Diligent mosquito-bite protection is recommended in all areas with malaria, even where the risk is low.
Chemoprophylaxis is recommended additionally for travel in all high-risk regions.
Standby emergency treatment is discussed for special risk situations when traveling to moderate risk regions.
Fever above 37.5 degrees Celsius on axillary or tympanic measurement: see a doctor or hospital immediately and have a blood test for malaria, whether or not chemoprophylaxis was used.
Atovaquone/Proguanil: 1 tablet daily. Start 1 day before travel. Continue during travel. End 7 days after travel.
Mefloquine: 1 tablet (250 mg) per week. Start 1 to 2 weeks before travel. End 4 weeks after travel.
Doxycycline: 1 tablet (100 mg) daily. Start 1 to 2 days before travel. End 4 weeks after travel.
Contraindications noted on the sheet: atovaquone/proguanil pregnancy (relative); mefloquine epilepsy and any past or present psychiatric disorder; doxycycline pregnancy and children under 8 years.
Tablets with or after food. Adult dosages. Malaria may occur from 7 days after entering an endemic area.
The dosages on this sheet are checked against the Swiss professional information before teaching.
