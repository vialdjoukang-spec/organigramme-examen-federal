Paediatric patients are defined as persons aged 0–18 years of age. In these Guidelines, we use the term children to encompass all age categories. When distinguishing between age groups, we use the word infant for neonates and children up to 1 year.

Give five initial rescue breaths.

Continue CPR. Use a compression-to-ventilation ratio of 15:2 if you are specifically trained in PBLS to the level of the ERC PBLS-course or equivalent, otherwise use a ratio of 30:2.

Use the two-thumb encircling method for chest compressions in infants.

Depress the chest by at least one third of the anteroposterior dimension. Use the adult depth recommendation of 5–6 cm in adolescents and do not exceed a depth of 6 cm at any age.

Rate of 100–120 min.

Use the head tilt chin lift manoeuvre to open the airway, assess breathing and look for signs of life for no longer than 10 s.

Obtain vascular access and give adrenaline IV/IO (10 µg kg, max 1 mg) as soon as possible followed by a flush. Repeat adrenaline IV/IO every 4 min (i.e. every other 2 min cycle) unless being guided by intra-arterial blood pressure monitoring.

The anteroposterior position for defibrillation pads is recommended for all infants and children weighing less than 25 kg.

Half of all paediatric out-of-hospital cardiac arrests have a distinguishable reversible cause with hypoxia being the most prevalent of these.

In previously healthy children aim for an SpO2 of 94–98 %.

Avoid fever for at least 72 h after cardiac arrest.

No single modality can be used in isolation for prognostication with high accuracy.

Fluid boluses 10 ml kg for major haemorrhage, in the table of hypovolaemia.

Recognise cardiac arrest: no signs of life and or bradycardia under 60 per minute with poor perfusion, as on the paediatric advanced life support algorithm read the same day.

Give adrenaline (10 µg kg, max 1 mg) and amiodarone (5 mg kg, max 300 mg) IV/IO immediately after the third shock. A second dose of amiodarone (5 mg kg, max 150 mg) after the fifth shock.

These milligram lines of amiodarone are the European guideline. They are not an Swiss information professionnelle.

HOTT: Hypotension, Oxygenation (hypoxia), Tension pneumothorax and cardiac Tamponade.
