# Acute respiratory infection and/or influenza-like illness in adults

de en fr

Collapse all

## Definition and disease spectrum

An acute illness (present for 21 days or less) affecting the respiratory tract with symptoms such as cough, sore throat, fever, sputum production, breathlessness, wheeze or chest discomfort or pain, and no alternative explanation.

* If sore throat predominates -> see [pharyngitis guideline](https://ssi.guidelines.ch/guideline/2408/de "Pharyngitis")
* If ear pain predominates -> see [otitis media guideline](https://ssi.guidelines.ch/guideline/2406/de "Akute Otitis media (AOM)")
* If localized sinus pain predominates -> see [sinusitis guideline](https://ssi.guidelines.ch/guideline/2410/de "Akute bakterielle Sinusitis")

In the case of influenza, generalised symptoms (headache, malaise, myalgia, arthralgia) and sometimes gastrointestinal symptoms may predominate over respiratory symptoms. Atypical presentations are possible, particularly among older individuals.

## Severity criteria – red flags

Consider hospital admission if

* Poor overall clinical impression
* Systolic blooc pressure < 90 mmHg
* Tachypnea (≥ 30 breaths/min.)
* Oxygen saturation < 92%
* Haemoptysis
* Stridor
* Severe pulmonary disease
* Severe immunodeficiency

## Tests

**A laboratory test is always recommended before initiation of any antiviral or antibiotic treatment.**

### **Imaging**

* Given the limited diagnostic performance of chest X-rays to identify pulmonary consolidation, radiology is **not** routinely recommended for patients managed in the ambulatory setting.
* Lung ultrasound is an appropriate diagnostic alternative if performed by a trained operator.

### **Test for inflammatory markers to support the decision of antibiotic prescription**

* **DO NOT TEST** : Patients with acute cough and at least 2 of the following criteria:
  
    + nasal discharge
    + sore throat or hoarseness
    + mild fever (<38.5°C)
  
  and no breathlessness, tachycardia, or crackles on auscultation.
  
  These patients can be considered as very  unlikely to benefit from antibiotic prescription (<1-3%). They should not be tested for an inflammatory marker.

* **TEST** : Patients with acute cough and at least one of the following criteria:
  
    + measured fever ( ≥38.5°C rectal/ear) for >3 days
    + dyspnea/breathlessness
    + tachypnea (respiratory rate ≥22/minute)
    + focal abnormal auscultation
    + pulse rate ≥100 /minute
  
  These patients have an intermediate to high chance of benefitting from an antibiotic treatment and who should be tested for an inflammatory marker.
  
  Antibiotics should be considered in these patients if CRP ≥100 mg/L (or procalcitonin\* ≥0.25 μg/L), whereas symptomatic treatment may be preferred in patients with CRP <50 mg/L (or procalcitonin <0.25 μg/L).
* In case of doubt or incomplete criteria (e.g. CRP between 50 and 100 mg/L), physicians are advised to **share decision** with the patient.
  
    + Shared decision can use [**CEST** **= Clinical encounter support tool**](https://www.biham.unibe.ch/shared_decision_making/therapy/cough/index_eng.html) , a tool supporting shared decision with the patient  developed by the Institute of Primary health Care of Bern (BIHAM).

\*As of June 2025, point-of-care procalcitonin is not included in the analysis list as test for primary care practices and is not reimbursed in this setting.

### **Microbiological testing**

* A specific microbiological test (for influenza or SARS-CoV-2) is recommended only if a specific antiviral prescription is considered.
* The benefit/risk and costs/benefit ratio of the treatment and the test should be balanced for each prescription (“will the result change clinical management?”). The presence of an epidemiological link should also be included in the decision to test. Current data on respiratory viruses isolated in respiratory samples from the Sentinella outpatient network can be found on the IDD dashboard of the Federal Office of Public Health ( [Portail d’information de l’OFSP sur les maladies transmissibles (admin.ch)](https://www.idd.bag.admin.ch/topics/respiratory-pathogens/overview) ).
* The turn-around time of the test should also be taken in account, as it may delay the prescription of the treatment.

#### **Which microbiological diagnostic test?**

#### **_For influenza_**

For influenza, use a molecular test (PCR). Molecular point-of-care tests may be used to reduce turn-around time. However, their costs and availability may reduce their use.

Rapid antigenic tests are not recommended for influenza testing in adults because of limited diagnostic performances in this population (due to underlying preexisting immunity).

#### **_For SARS-CoV-2_**

Molecular testing is recommended for SARS-CoV-2 if test result impacts clinical management in high-risk patients. See [COVID-19 SSI guidelines](https://ssi.guidelines.ch/guideline/3352/de/31357 "SARS-CoV-2 /COVID-19 - Antiviral and immunomodulatory treatment considerations for early therapy and advanced disease")

Microbiological tests other than those for influenza or SARS-CoV-2 (see recommendations below) are usually not recommended for outpatients. Tests for _Bordetella pertussis_ can be considered in specific epidemic contexts to reduce risk of transmission.

## Treatment

### **Symptomatic treatment**

Based on a literature review performed by the Berner Institute for Primary Care:

* **NSAIDs** like Ibuprofen are for adult patients with fever, head- and/or muscle ache.
* **Antitussives** / opioids: Dextromethorphan might relieve an unproductive cough, but its efficacy is debated. According to the NICE-Guidelines codeine is not more effective than placebo.
* **Bronchodilators** should only be prescribed if infection aggravates cough caused by an underlying airways disease, e.g. asthma.
* **Oral steroids** : no evidence was found to support the use of corticosteroids for URTI/LRTI in patients with clinically unrecognised asthma.
* **Local steroids** : there is insufficient evidence to recommend the routine use of inhaled corticosteroids.
* **Phytotherapeutics** / Honey: A combination of ivy and thyme may relieve cough. Echinacea is not effective. Honey may decrease the frequency and severity of cough and improve sleep quality.
* **Antihistamines** and decongestants provide little clinical benefit.
* **Mucolytic agents** : Acetylcysteine showed some benefit (e.g. reduction of cough at day seven), but level of evidence is low

### **Empirical antibiotics**

If the patient is considered as likely to benefit from antibiotics, [refer to the pneumonia guidelines](https://ssi.guidelines.ch/guideline/3007/fr "Pneumonie / Pneumonie acquise en communauté") to select the appropriate molecule:

**Outpatients**

* without comorbidities: Amoxicillin 1 g q8 h p.o., maximum daily dose: 3 g
* with comorbidities: Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid 1 g q8 po
* Alternatives:
  
    + Age ≥8 years without comorbidities: Doxycycline 100 mg q12h po or Azithromycin 500 mg q24h or Clarithromycin 500 mg q12h p.o
    + Older patients or patients with comorbidities, including COPD: Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid 1 g per 8h po
    + Older patients or those with co-morbidities including COPD, and allergy to penicillin: Azithromycin 500 mg per 24h p.o or Clarithromycin 500 mg per 12h po (alternative: Levofloxacin 500 mg per 12 h p.o or Moxifloxacin 400 mg per 24h p.o)

**Duration of treatment**

* 2-3 days after temperature normalization/clinical stabilization; in principle 5 days
* A shorter course of treatment is possible in cases of mild/moderate pneumonia with a rapidly favorable clinical course

Antibiotic therapy is not recommended for patients infected with influenza or SARS-CoV-2 who have a low suspicion of bacterial superinfection, due to the risk of resistance and potential side effects.

### **Influenza: when to prescribe antivirals**

_Treatment of uncomplicated influenza in high-risk\* outpatients_ , or high-risk\* patients with nosocomial infections presenting influenza-like illness for less than 48 hours:

* Baloxavir single dose depending on the weight: 40 mg if >80 kg; 80 mg if >80 kg.
* Not recommended for pregnant women (no existing safety data in this population).

This treatment reduced the duration of the symptoms of 1 day. Baloxavir administered within 48 hours of symptoms in high-risk individuals may reduce the risk of hospitalization. No effect on mortality has been observed in clinical trials.

Antiviral treatments are not generally covered by the health insurance in Switzerland. Patients should be informed of the cost of the treatment course, as well as the existing limited evidence in outpatient use before prescription. “Ex factory” cost of a baloxavir treatment course = 120 -150 CHF. Oseltamivir: around 50 CHF for a 5 day-treatment course.

\*Patients at high-risk of developing severe illness:

* Age  ≥65 years
* Immunosuppression
* Cardiovascular disease
* Neurological disease
* Chronic respiratory disease

Or person of any age with multiple risk factors, including malignancy, diabetes, …

Expert opinion from the SSI guidelines ILI group: considering that the current evidence is not very strong, that treatment is not reimbursed in Switzerland, and that keeping the 48h to initiate treatment is difficult in real life, we do not recommend routine use of baloxavir in high risk patients in the ambulatory setting, and would reserve its use for very high-risk patients (≥85 years old or immunocompromised).

As resistance frequently appears under treatment, careful use of baloxavir should be made in the immunocompromised population. Combination therapy with oseltamivir should be discussed in this population.

_Treatment of severe influenza disease (= requiring hospitalization)._

Hospitalization may be required for exacerbation of underlying disease, severe pneumonia, sepsis or critical disease.

* In this population, the effect of oseltamivir on mortality is very uncertain. Early treatment may decrease the length of hospitalization of one day. There is not enough existing data on baloxavir.
* There is no benefit of the combination (oseltamivir + baloxavir) therapy in this population.
* Oseltamivir may be prescribed for 5 days, if symptoms are ongoing for less than 48 hours
* In patients with critical disease and symptoms evolving for more than 48 hours, observational data suggest a mortality benefit.

Infectious diseases specialist should be contacted in case of underlying immunosuppression to discuss combination therapy of NAI (oseltamivir) + baloxavir (expert opinion).

## Prevention

### **Vaccinations recommended in the context of influenza-like illnesses**

There are recommendations for vaccination against several diseases causing influenza like-illnesses ( _Bordetella pertussis_ , influenza, SARS-CoV-2, _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ (PCV), RSV). For complete recommendations, please refer to the latest Swiss vaccination plan ( [Swiss vaccination plan](https://backend.bag.admin.ch/fileservice/sdweb-docs-prod-bagadminch-files/files/2025/05/08/5f1f0792-3b3d-4aab-9ed8-35ffe6cfd44b.pdf) ).

### **Prevention/Chemoprophylaxis**

For influenza :

* Administration of a treatment reduces by 5% the relative risk of confirmed laboratory influenza, without any demonstrated effect on mortality.
* Post-exposure therapy may be offered to extremely high-risk individuals (≥ 85 years old or individuals with multiple risk-factors), for example in the context of nursing homes, who have been in contact with a positive case within the first 48 hours.
  
    + Treatment :
        
        - Baloxavir : single dose depending on the weight: 40 mg if >80 kg; 80 mg if >80 kg.
        - Oseltamivir depending on renal clearance, for 10 days
