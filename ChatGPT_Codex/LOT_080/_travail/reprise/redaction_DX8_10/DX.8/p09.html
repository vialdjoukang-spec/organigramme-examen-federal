Muscle MRI, used in many studies on camptocormia in PD, has its relevant limitations, consisting mostly of high cost, contraindications (such as the presence of some implantable electronic devices, metallic foreign bodies, etc. [48]), and time of investigation. MRI usually requires a laying position when camptocormia alleviates. A more cost-effective and less limiting assessment method represents muscle ultrasonography. Yilmaz et al. (2023) [49] used ultrasonography on age and sex-matched 17 PD patients with camptocormia, 19 PD patients without camptocormia and 18 healthy controls. Significantly thinner lumbar paravertebral muscles in the PD group with camptocormia were identified, with an increase of muscle echogenicity. The ultrasonographic assessment of the muscle could be useful for a prospective study exploring the evolution of camptocormia in PD.
